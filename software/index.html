Software

MacCallum designs and builds the software his works run on, as he has throughout his career, first at UC Berkeley’s Center for New Music and Audio Technologies (CNMAT) and now as an engineer on Max and Max for Live at Cycling ’74 and Ableton. The music drives the software, and the software, in turn, the music.

asoma 2019–

The successor to odot, asoma adds computation to OpenSoundControl: the state of a running program can be paused on a laptop and resumed on a microcontroller, so that a heterogeneous collection of cameras, computers, sensors, and embedded hosts can be treated as a single instrument. Independent of his work at Cycling ’74; first public release planned for 2027.

odot 2007–

A programming environment for OpenSoundControl, co-designed with Adrian Freed at CNMAT. MacCallum wrote the C implementation, the expression language, and much of the Max library. odot provides a layer between OSC nodes so that they can be composed together dynamically: instruments, sensors, cameras, and computers that share no clock, native language, or vendor can be treated as a single system. In continuous use since 2007, and taught at UC Berkeley, the Synthesis Center at Arizona State University, and the Hochschule für Musik und Theater Hamburg.

Tools for polytemporal music 2008–2015

Software for composing and performing music in independent, smoothly varying tempos: Timewarp, and tempo-curving tools developed with Jean Bresson. Used in Aberration, …delicate texture of time…, Hyphos, and Limn, in works by Edmund Campion, and in several works with Teoma Naccarato. The click tracks for each piece are on its page.

Papers: Timewarp, ICMC 2010, Tempo curving, SMC 2015

Camera Cage 2014–

The performance system built with Teoma Naccarato for the III and Im/mediations works: biosensing, multi-camera live-feed and live-stream video, and networked audio. At its largest, for III: Once Returned, 12 cameras, 9 computers, and 3 networks running continuously for 72 hours.

rtecg

Real-time feature detection for the electrocardiogram: an implementation of the Pan–Tompkins algorithm.

Software for the ZM4 wavefield synthesis array, HfMT Hamburg 2019–2022

Core development since 2022, including the built-in OpenSoundControl support released in Max 9 (2024); Max for Live with Ableton since 2025.
