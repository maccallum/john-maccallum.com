John MacCallum is a composer, media artist, and software engineer. Born in California and trained in music composition and computer science, he lived in Berlin from 2019 to 2025 and is now based in Falmouth, UK. His work uses technology to probe our relationship to time. At its center is the heart, as timekeeper, material performer, and world-builder. For over a decade that work has been made in collaboration with choreographer and media artist Teoma Naccarato: extended-duration performances inside high-channel-count real-time audio/visual systems that put philosophical pressure on notions of representation, boundaries, and binaries. He designs and builds the software these systems run on, as he has throughout his career, first at UC Berkeley’s Center for New Music and Audio Technologies and now as an engineer on Max and Max for Live at Cycling ’74 and Ableton; the music drives the software, and the software, in turn, the music. He holds degrees in music composition from UC Berkeley (PhD), McGill, and the University of the Pacific.

Works

Since 2014, most of the work has been made with choreographer Teoma Naccarato, from 2025 as Friction Field Collaborative. Choreography, direction, and video are Naccarato’s; sound, live electronics, and the sensing, video, and network systems are MacCallum’s. Both perform.

Software

odot
A programming environment for OpenSoundControl, co-designed with Adrian Freed at UC Berkeley’s Center for New Music and Audio Technologies (CNMAT). MacCallum wrote the C implementation, the expression language, and much of the Max library. Taught at Berkeley, Arizona State University, and the Hochschule für Musik und Theater Hamburg.
asoma
The successor to odot: a distributed system of sensors, computers, and cameras addressed as a single instrument. An independent project; first public release planned for 2027.
rtecg
Real-time feature detection for the electrocardiogram, an implementation of the Pan–Tompkins algorithm.
